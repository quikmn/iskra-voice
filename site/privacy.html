Privacy Policy

Last updated 11 October 2026

Iskra is built so that as little as possible about you ends up anywhere. This page explains what is stored, where, who can see it, and how to get rid of it.

The short version
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking. Not in the apps, not on the websites.
  • No email, no phone number, no password. Your account is a key on your devices.
  • There's no central Iskra database. Each server you join stores your profile and what you post there, and is run by its owner.
  • Direct messages, group chats and their calls are end-to-end encrypted. Servers only pass along ciphertext.
  • You can delete your account from the app, on every server at once.

1. Who is responsible for your data

Iskra isn't one service: it's software that many people run. So who is responsible (the "controller" under the GDPR) depends on where your data is:

  • Each server's owner is responsible for the data on their server: your profile there, your messages, files and so on. If you want something from a server, ask its owner. They can see everything on their server that isn't end-to-end encrypted.
  • Viktor Lundgren ("From a Spark"), Sweden, is responsible for the websites iskra.foo and app.iskra.foo, and for the servers he runs, such as chat.iskra.foo. Contact: spark@iskra.foo. In this policy, "we" means him.

We make the apps, but we don't receive your chats, your contacts or your usage. The apps don't send anything to us.

2. On your device

  • Your account keys are created on your device and never leave it unencrypted. In the desktop app they're protected by your operating system's keychain (Windows DPAPI, macOS Keychain, or GNOME Keyring / KWallet on Linux). In the browser and the phone app they're stored in the browser's storage (IndexedDB), encrypted with a key the browser keeps and never reveals. You can add an app lock passphrase on top (Settings → App lock).
  • Your decrypted direct messages and group chats, your settings, and the list of servers you've joined are stored on the device, encrypted in the same way.
  • Settings that sync between your devices are stored on your servers end-to-end encrypted, so a server can't read them or see which other servers you're on.
  • Your recovery key is shown to you once and whenever you ask for it. Where you keep it is up to you.
  • Activity status ("Playing X", desktop app only) is off until you turn it on. Games are recognised on your computer; only the game's name is sent to your servers, never the list of running programs.

3. On the servers you join

When you join a server, it stores what it needs to run your account there:

  • Your account's public key (your identity) and the public keys and names of your devices.
  • Your profile: display name, avatar, bio, pronouns, profile colour, custom status, and your profile page if you make one. Plus your nickname, roles and when you joined, and which server rules you accepted.
  • What you post: messages, edits, files, images, voice messages, reactions, polls and votes, pins, threads, forum posts, events you create or are interested in, emoji, stickers and sounds you add.
  • Settings for that server, such as notification levels, and your block list (so the server can stop DMs and calls from people you've blocked; nobody else sees it).
  • Push subscriptions for the devices where you turned notifications on (see Notifications).
  • Your presence (online, idle, in voice) is kept in memory only while you're connected.

Server channels, voice channels and files are not end-to-end encrypted: whoever runs the server can read them, and so can the members allowed into each channel. Servers use HTTPS when the owner has turned it on (it's automatic with "Open to the internet").

A server sees your IP address while you're connected, because it needs it to talk to you. Iskra's server doesn't store IP addresses in its database and doesn't log requests by default. Servers also keep daily backups (the last 7 days by default) so the owner can undo mistakes.

Links in messages get a preview (title and description) that the server fetches, not your device, so the website sees the server's address instead of yours.

4. Direct messages, group chats and calls

Direct messages and group chats, including their pictures, files and voice messages, are end-to-end encrypted: only the people in the conversation can read them. Voice and video calls in DMs and group chats are end-to-end encrypted too. You can read the details in our security notes.

A server that carries them stores only the ciphertext and the metadata needed to deliver it: who sent what to whom, when, and how big it is, plus group membership. It also knows who calls whom, when and for how long. It can't read the content or listen to the call.

5. Voice and video

Voice and video go through a voice service (LiveKit) that runs alongside each server, on the same machine, under the server owner's control. It sees your IP address while you're in a call. In voice channels and stages the audio and video aren't end-to-end encrypted (the server could in principle access them); in DM and group calls they are. Iskra doesn't record calls.

When a server is open to the internet, its voice service may ask a public STUN server (by default one run by Google) for the server's own public address. This reveals the server's address, not yours.

6. Reports and moderation

  • When you report a message, the server keeps a copy of it as it was when you reported it (text and attachments), with your reason and note, so moderators can see it even if it's deleted.
  • When you report from a DM or group chat, only the messages you choose to share are sent to that server's moderators. The server can't check them against the encrypted originals, and moderators are told so.
  • Your identity as the reporter is visible to the server's moderators, but never to the person you reported.
  • Moderators' actions (deleting messages, timeouts, kicks, bans, role changes) are recorded in the server's audit log, with names, for 90 days. Resolved reports are deleted 90 days after they're resolved.
  • If you email a safety concern to spark@iskra.foo, we keep the email for as long as we need it to handle the concern, and we may pass illegal content to the authorities.

7. Notifications

If you turn on notifications (Settings → Notifications, per device), each server sends them through your browser's push service: Google (Chrome, Edge on Android, the Android app), Mozilla (Firefox), Microsoft (Edge on Windows) or Apple (Safari, iPhone and iPad). This uses the Web Push standard: every notification is encrypted to your device, so the push service can't read it, but it does see that a notification was sent to your device, and when.

  • DM and group chat notifications never contain the message, just "New message from X".
  • Mentions and replies in server channels include the sender's name, the channel and a short excerpt (up to 140 characters).

The Android app runs the web app in Chrome, so its notifications come through Google's push service as well. If we release native phone apps later, their notifications would go through Apple's (APNs) or Google's (FCM) notification services in the same content-free way for DMs; we'll update this page first.

9. The websites

iskra.foo and app.iskra.foo are static sites hosted on Cloudflare Pages. Like any web host, Cloudflare processes your IP address and request details to deliver the pages and protect them from attacks, and keeps short-lived logs. We don't add analytics, cookies or trackers to either site.

  • app.iskra.foo only delivers the web app's files. Once loaded, the app talks straight to the servers you join; Cloudflare doesn't see your chats.
  • iskra.foo (this website) loads its fonts from Google Fonts, so Google sees your IP address when you visit it. The app itself doesn't use Google Fonts.
  • Links to Ko-fi, PayPal and GitHub take you to those sites, under their own privacy policies.

Some of these companies (Cloudflare, Google, GitHub, and some push services) are based in the United States. They transfer data under the EU's approved safeguards, such as the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses.

10. How long things are kept

  • Your messages and files stay on a server until you or a moderator delete them, the server's owner removes them, or you delete your account (and choose to delete your messages).
  • Uploads that aren't used by any message, avatar or the like are deleted after 24 hours.
  • Encrypted DM envelopes stay on a server so your other devices can fetch them; deleting your account removes your copies. The short-lived keys that protect them are deleted about five weeks after use, after which old envelopes can't be decrypted even with your device keys.
  • Audit log entries: 90 days. Resolved reports: 90 days after they're resolved.
  • Daily server backups: 7 days by default (the owner can choose 1 to 30).
  • Ended or cancelled events: 30 days.

11. Deleting your data

You can delete things yourself: edit your profile, delete your own messages, remove a device, or leave a server. To remove everything at once, use Delete account in Settings → Account. It:

  • removes your profile, avatar, nickname, roles and settings on every server you're in;
  • optionally deletes all your messages on those servers;
  • deletes the server copies of your encrypted DMs and group chats;
  • revokes all your devices, and wipes Iskra from the device you're using.

What isn't deleted: messages already on other people's devices (for example the DMs you sent them) stay with them. Moderators may keep reports and the audit log for safety, with your name removed. Servers that are offline at the time are reached by your other devices when they next connect, or you can ask their owners. See How to delete your account for step-by-step instructions and what to do if you've lost your devices.

12. Children

Iskra is for people aged 13 or over, or the age at which you can consent to online services yourself in your country if that's higher (13 in Sweden; up to 16 in some EU countries). Below that age, you may only use it with a parent's or guardian's permission where the law allows. If you believe a child's data is on a server we run without that permission, email spark@iskra.foo and we'll delete it.

13. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to how it's used, and to get it in a portable form. Much of this you can do yourself in the app. For a server, ask its owner; for iskra.foo, app.iskra.foo and servers we run, email us.

Why we process data on what we run: to provide the service you asked for (your account, messages and calls on our server), and our legitimate interest in keeping the sites and servers working, secure and free of abuse. Where the law requires it, we may share information with authorities.

You can complain to the Swedish data protection authority, Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY), at imy.se, or to the authority where you live. We'd appreciate the chance to fix it first.
