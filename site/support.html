Help & Support

Last updated 11 October 2026

Answers to the common questions, how to report something, and how to reach us.

Getting started

How do I join a server?

Ask someone on the server for an invite link (they get one from Invite friends). Open the link: it opens Iskra in your browser, or in the desktop app if you have it. Pick a name and you're in. No email or password needed.

You can also open app.iskra.foo or the desktop app, choose Join a server and paste the link.

How do I start my own server?

In the desktop app, click Create a server and pick a name. The server runs on your computer while the app is open, and you're its owner. Click Invite friends to get a link.

Friends on your home network can join right away. For friends elsewhere, turn on Settings → Open to the internet: it opens the ports on your router and sets up HTTPS for you. For a server that's always on, run the standalone server on a spare computer or a rented one.

Everything is explained in the hosting guide.

Can I use Iskra on my phone?

Yes: open app.iskra.foo in your phone's browser and add it to your home screen. On iPhone and iPad, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen (notifications only work from the Home Screen app). The server you join needs HTTPS, which "Open to the internet" sets up.

Your account and devices

What's the recovery key, and why does it matter?

Your account lives on your devices, not on a server, and there's no password. The recovery key is how you get your account back on a new device, or after clearing your browser or losing your phone. Find it in Settings → Recovery key and keep it somewhere safe, like a password manager.

Nobody can recover it for you, not us and not a server owner. And anyone who has it can use your account, so never share it. Nobody from Iskra or a server will ever ask you for it.

How do I use my account on a new phone or computer?

The easiest way is device linking: on a device where you're signed in, open Settings → Devices → Link a device, then scan the QR code (or type the code) on the new device. Both devices show a six-digit number to compare before anything is sent. Your account goes straight from one device to the other, encrypted.

No other device at hand? On the new device choose I already have Iskra, then Use my recovery key, and paste your key. Then open an invite link to each of your servers to get back in.

I lost a device. What should I do?

On another device, open Settings → Devices and remove the lost one. It's signed out for good. If you're worried someone has your recovery key, you can replace your account key in the app; your friends will see that your safety number changed.

How do I leave a server or sign out?
  • Hide a server from your list: open the server's settings, Overview → Remove server. You stay a member and can come back with any invite link.
  • Sign out of this device: Settings → Devices → Sign out of this device. Tick Also leave my servers to leave them on every device. Make sure you've saved your recovery key if this is your only device.
  • Leave Iskra completely: delete your account.

Notifications

I don't get notifications
  • Turn them on for each device in Settings → Notifications, and allow them when your browser or system asks.
  • If it says notifications are blocked, allow them for Iskra in your browser's site settings (or your phone's settings), then try again.
  • On iPhone and iPad, notifications only work in the app added to the Home Screen.
  • On phones, the server needs HTTPS ("Open to the internet" sets it up).
  • You won't get a notification on a device you're actively using, and a muted server or channel doesn't notify you (right-click it, or long-press on a phone, to check).

Voice and video

Nobody can hear me, or I can't hear anyone
  • Check that Iskra may use your microphone (browser site settings, or your system's privacy settings), and pick the right devices in Settings → Voice & audio.
  • Make sure you're not muted or deafened, and that push-to-talk isn't on by mistake.
  • If voice works at home but not for friends elsewhere, the server's voice ports need to be reachable: 7881/tcp and 7882/udp. "Open to the internet" opens them on most routers; otherwise forward them by hand (see the ports in the hosting guide).
  • Strict networks (some workplaces, schools and VPNs) can block voice. Try another network to check.
Video or screen sharing doesn't work

Allow camera or screen recording for Iskra (on macOS: System Settings → Privacy & Security). Calls in DMs and group chats are end-to-end encrypted, and a browser that can't encrypt media can't join them: use an up-to-date browser or the desktop app.

Safety: reporting and blocking

  • Report a message or a person: open the message's menu, or the person's menu, and choose Report. It goes to that server's moderators, who can delete it, time the person out, kick or ban them. The reported person never sees who reported them.
  • In DMs and group chats (end-to-end encrypted), you can choose messages to share with the moderators of a server you both are on.
  • Block someone: from their menu, choose Block. It stops their DMs and calls to you on every server you share, and their messages stop notifying you. Manage your list in Settings → Blocked people.
Report a safety concern to the Iskra team

For illegal content, a danger to someone, a server whose moderators ignore reports, or anything on a server we run, email spark@iskra.foo with the subject "Safety concern". Include the server's address and what happened (screenshots help). If someone is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number first (112 in the EU).

Read the community guidelines for what isn't allowed on Iskra.

Bugs and ideas

Found a bug, or have an idea for Iskra? Open an issue on GitHub. Say which app you use (desktop, web, phone), its version, and what you did. Please don't put private messages, keys or invite links in a public issue.

Found a security problem? Email spark@iskra.foo instead of opening a public issue.

Contact

Iskra is made by Viktor Lundgren ("From a Spark") in Sweden. Email spark@iskra.foo. We can't help with servers we don't run (we can't see them); for those, ask the server's owner.
