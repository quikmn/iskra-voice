Iskra and Discord compared

A Discord alternative you own

Facts about Discord checked on 25 November 2026, with sources

Iskra is voice and text chat for friend groups, like Discord, on a server that you or a friend runs. Nobody asks for your ID, your face, your email or your phone number, and nothing is sold as a subscription.

The short version
  • You own the server. It is a folder on your computer or on one you rent. Messages and files are stored there, not with a company.
  • No ID checks. No ID document, no face scan, no email address, no phone number, no password.
  • Nothing behind a paywall. Big uploads, full-quality screen sharing, custom emoji, the soundboard, profiles and themes are free for everyone.
  • Private chats are end-to-end encrypted. Direct messages, group chats and the calls in them.
  • No ads, and the apps have no analytics.
  • It is an alpha, and Discord is ahead in several ways. We list them below.

ID checks and face scans

Discord

On 9 February 2026 Discord announced that every account would get a "teen-appropriate experience" by default, and that people would confirm they are adults with facial age estimation or an identity document. After criticism it postponed the change. The worldwide rollout began on 22 September 2026.

  • Each account is placed in one of three groups: adult, teen or unconfirmed. Discord says more than 90% of accounts are placed automatically from account signals and never have to prove their age.
  • An account that is not confirmed as adult cannot open age-restricted servers and channels, sees sensitive images blurred, and cannot change some safety settings.
  • The ways to confirm include a video selfie processed on the device, a scan of an ID, a payment card, and the age range from the phone's app store. Discord says its partners return only an age group and delete what they receive.
  • In October 2025 Discord said that ID photos of about 70,000 users may have been exposed in a breach at a company that handled its customer support. Discord says it no longer works with that company.

Iskra

Iskra has no ID check, and nothing to attach one to. There is no central account: your account is a key that the app makes on your device. Iskra never asks for an ID document, a selfie, a payment card, an email address or a phone number.

  • Our terms say you must be 13 or older, or the minimum age in your country. Nobody checks that with a document.
  • You get into a server with an invite link from someone in it. The owner decides who gets one, can ask questions before letting people in, and can set a higher age for their server.
  • If you run a server, you are responsible for it and for following the law where you live.

What is free

Discord is free to use and sells two subscriptions. On discord.com/nitro Nitro Basic costs $2.99 a month and Nitro $9.99 a month in the US. They add larger uploads (50 MB with Nitro Basic; the free limit rose from 10 MB to 20 MB on 13 August 2026), HD streaming, using a server's emoji anywhere, animated avatars, profile themes and more.

Iskra has no paid tier. Everything it can do is there for everyone:

  • Uploads up to 512 MB per file by default. The server's owner can raise or lower the limit, because it is their disk.
  • Screen sharing at your screen's own resolution and 60 fps, with system audio.
  • Custom emoji, animated emoji, stickers and a soundboard on every server.
  • Profiles: avatar, bio, pronouns, accent colour and a profile page you design yourself.
  • Every app theme, and you can write your own.

Iskra is paid for by donations. A server needs a computer that stays on. Your own computer costs nothing extra. A small rented server costs about $6 a month at DigitalOcean or about €4 at Hetzner, paid to them. The hosting guide explains each way.

Who owns the server and the data

A Discord server is a space on Discord's computers. Discord stores its messages and files, sets the rules, and can change or close it.

An Iskra server is a program that you or a friend runs. Settings, messages, members and uploads are in one folder on that computer.

  • Press Create a server in the desktop app and it runs on your computer, voice included.
  • The server makes a backup every day. You can download an encrypted backup, restore it on another computer, and members follow the server to its new address.
  • There is no central Iskra database. Servers send nothing to us, and we cannot read, moderate or close a server we don't run.

Encryption

Discord made voice and video calls end-to-end encrypted for everyone in 2026: calls in direct messages, group chats and voice channels, and Go Live streams. Stage channels are the exception. Text messages are not end-to-end encrypted, and Discord has said it has no current plans to change that.

In Iskra:

  • Direct messages and group chats are always end-to-end encrypted, with their files, voice messages and calls. The server passes them along and cannot read them.
  • Server channels are encrypted on the way and can be read by whoever runs the server: you or your friend. We say that plainly.
  • A private channel of up to 50 people can be made end-to-end encrypted, text and voice. Then the server cannot read it either.

Ads and analytics

Discord sells advertising: sponsored Quests, video ads, and since 29 September 2026 an Ads Manager for advertisers.

Iskra has no ads. The apps contain no analytics, no advertising code and no crash reporting, and they connect only to the servers you join. Who Iskra talks to lists every connection. This website counts page views as daily totals, without cookies and without identifying anyone; the privacy policy says exactly what is counted.

Source code

Discord's apps and servers are closed source.

All of Iskra's code is public on GitHub: the apps, the server and the protocol. It is source-available under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0. Moving your group over

  1. Start a server. Download the desktop app, press Create a server and pick a starting point. It runs on your computer. For a server that is always on, rent a small one from the same dialog.
  2. Bring your history, if you want it. Export your Discord channels with the third-party tool DiscordChatExporter in JSON format, then use Server settings → Import from Discord. Messages keep their dates, authors' names and pictures, replies, pins, files and reaction counts, and are marked as imported.
  3. Set up roles. Roles and permissions can't be exported from Discord, so make them again. Direct messages are not imported.
  4. Send an invite link. Friends click it, pick a name and are in. No sign-up.
  5. Point your tools at it. Anything that posts to a Discord webhook can post to an Iskra webhook address. Discord bots do not run on Iskra; it has built-in bots and its own bot SDK.

What Discord does better, and what Iskra doesn't have yet

Iskra is an alpha made by one person. Discord is a finished product with a large team. Check this list before you move a group.

  • Nobody has to host Discord. An Iskra server needs a computer that stays on. If it runs on your PC and the PC is off, the server is off.
  • Phones. Discord has finished phone apps with push notifications. Iskra's Android and iPhone apps are test versions and notify you only while they are open. For notifications while the app is closed, use the web app in your phone's browser, added to the home screen.
  • Screen sharing from a phone. You can share your screen from the desktop app and from desktop browsers, not from a phone.
  • Everyone is already on Discord. It has public communities, server discovery and one account for all of them. Iskra has no directory: you join a server with an invite link, and you can message people only if you share a server with them.
  • Bots and integrations. Discord has a very large bot ecosystem, an app directory and game integrations on consoles. Iskra has a few built-in bots, webhooks and an SDK for writing your own.
  • Size. Iskra is built for friend groups and small communities. It has not been run with tens of thousands of members in one server.
  • Calls in public channels. On Discord every voice channel is end-to-end encrypted. On Iskra that is true for direct messages, group chats and channels you make end-to-end encrypted; other voice channels are encrypted to your own server.
  • Moderation at scale. Discord has a trust and safety team. On Iskra the server's owner and moderators do it, with reports, AutoMod, timeouts, bans and an audit log.
  • You are responsible for your server. Its backups, its uptime, and following the law where you live.
  • Polish. Expect rough edges and bugs. Releases come often.

Questions

Does Iskra ask for ID, a face scan or my age?

No. Iskra never asks for an ID document, a selfie, a payment card, an email address or a phone number. There is no central account to attach them to: your account is a key made on your own device. Iskra's terms say you must be 13 or older, or the minimum age in your country. Nobody checks that with a document. The owner of a server decides who gets an invite and can set a higher age for their server.

Is Iskra free? What does it cost to run a server?

The apps and the server are free and there is no paid tier. A server needs a computer that stays on. That can be your own Windows, Mac or Linux computer, which costs nothing extra, or a small rented server at about 4 to 6 euros or dollars a month, paid to the hosting company, not to Iskra.

Is Iskra open source?

Iskra is source-available. All the code is public on GitHub under the PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0: you may read it, audit it, change it and run it for free for personal and noncommercial use. That licence is not an open-source licence as the Open Source Initiative defines it, because it does not allow commercial use.

Can I move my Discord server to Iskra?

You can bring the history of your channels: messages with their dates, replies, pins, files and reaction counts. Export the channels with the third-party tool DiscordChatExporter in JSON format and import them in Server settings → Import from Discord. Roles, permissions and direct messages do not come along. Members join the new server with an invite link and get a new Iskra identity.

Are Iskra messages end-to-end encrypted?

Direct messages, group chats and the voice and video calls in them are always end-to-end encrypted, with forward secrecy for direct messages. Server channels are encrypted on the way to the server and can be read by whoever runs that server. A private channel of up to 50 people can be made end-to-end encrypted, text and voice.

Who can read my messages on an Iskra server?

The members of the channel, and the person who runs the server, because channel messages are stored on their computer. The makers of Iskra cannot: there is no central Iskra database and servers send nothing to Iskra. Nobody but the people in the chat can read direct messages, group chats or end-to-end encrypted channels.

Do my friends need an account?

No. They open your invite link, pick a name and are in. The app makes a key on their device, and a recovery key lets them get back in on another device. There is no email, password or sign-up form.

Does Iskra work on phones?

Yes, with limits. There are Android and iPhone apps in testing and a web app at app.iskra.foo that can be added to the home screen. The phone apps notify you only while they are open; the web app can notify you while it is closed. Phones cannot share their screen or host a server.
