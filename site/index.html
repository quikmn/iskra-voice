Features

Everything you need.
Nothing you don't.

Here's what's in the alpha: voice with AI noise removal, live captions, stages and watch parties, text, forums, events and docs, screen share at full resolution and 60 fps, encrypted DMs, group chats, calls and private channels, roles, bots and profiles you design yourself, on a server you control.

One identity, made on your device

Pick a name and you're in. Your identity is a key created on your own device, stored encrypted, with an optional app lock. No email, no password, no signup form. A recovery key restores it on another device, and you can replace the key if it ever leaks.

Join with an invite link

Click a link, pick a name, you're talking. Server owners create invite links and share them however they like: a group chat, a text, an email.

Friends, status & activity

Add the people you hang out with as friends and message them directly. Set a status and a custom status, or go invisible, and let friends see the game you're playing (opt-in, desktop app). No global usernames to fight over.

End-to-end encrypted DMs

DMs are always end-to-end encrypted, with forward secrecy, using libsodium, a proven library. Pictures, files and voice messages too. No hand-made crypto. Compare safety numbers with a friend to make sure you're really talking to them.

Group chats & group calls

Chats with up to ten friends, with files and voice messages, and voice or video calls for the whole group. All end-to-end encrypted: the server only passes them along, and every change to who's in the group is signed by the person who made it.

Encrypted calls

Voice and video calls with a friend, right from your DMs. Every frame is encrypted on your device, so the server only passes along noise. Group calls work the same way.

Honest server channels

Server channels are encrypted on the way, and readable by whoever runs the server: you, or your friend. That's the honest deal, and we say it plainly. Want more? Make a small private channel (up to 50 people) end-to-end encrypted, text and voice, and not even the server can read it.

Voice Channels

Voice runs through an SFU (LiveKit), so big voice channels stay smooth instead of falling apart past a handful of people. Every voice channel has its own text chat. Mute, deafen, per-person volume, push-to-talk that works while a game has focus (desktop app), and clips: save the last seconds of a call, recorded on your own device, with everyone able to see who has clips on.

Stages & Watch Parties

Stages for talks and listening parties: everyone listens, and moderators bring people up to speak. Watch parties keep everyone in voice on the same frame of a video file, an upload or a YouTube video, with a party chat on the side.

Screen Share & Webcam

Turn on your camera or share your screen in a voice channel, at your screen's own resolution and 60 fps by default, with system audio (on Windows, without echoing the call itself), and no paywall. Blur your background or replace it, right on your device. The SFU means a share goes up once, not once per viewer.

New

Sound that just works

AI noise removal (DeepFilterNet3) takes out keyboards, fans and dogs. Voice detection opens your mic without clipping your first word, voices are evened out so nobody shouts or whispers, and you can clean up a friend's noisy mic on your end. Packets get lost, the audio doesn't. Admins can allow hi-fi stereo in a channel for music.

New

Live captions, on your device

Turn on captions in a call and see what people say, in English or Swedish. Speech is turned into text on your own device: no audio or text goes anywhere, and the captions are gone seconds later. The speech model comes from your own server, which fetches it once.

Text Channels

Markdown with headings, lists and highlighted code, spoilers, replies, edits with history, reactions, @mentions for people and roles, voice messages, forwarding, drafts and message links, uploads up to 512 MB by default with a gallery view, link previews, unread markers and notifications. The everyday stuff, done properly.

Forums & Events

Forum channels for questions, ideas and plans: posts with a title and tags, their own replies, search and "new" markers. Schedule game nights in a voice channel or anywhere else; friends tap Interested, get a reminder before it starts and add it to their calendar in one click.

New

Saved, reminders & send later

Save any message for later, or have Iskra remind you about it. Write a message now and send it in the morning. Every mention and reply waits in your inbox, and Catch up sums up what you missed, worked out on your own device.

New

Docs channels

A place for the things you keep explaining: house rules, guides, FAQs, campaign notes. Pages in a tree, with history, search and a "Start here" page, and you choose who can edit.

Organized your way

Channel categories you drag into place, server folders on the rail, per-channel notification levels and mutes (a small server tells you about every message, a big one only about @mentions, until you choose), nicknames per server, hide a server from your list or leave it, jump to the next unread with a shortcut, and a layout that follows you to every device. Works with the keyboard and screen readers, with readable contrast in every theme.

Threads, Pins & Search

Spin a thread off any message so side chats don't take over. Pin the good stuff. Search everything you can read, filter by person, files, links or date, and jump straight to the message.

Polls, Emoji, Stickers & GIFs

Settle game night with a poll. Add your server's own emoji and stickers, find a GIF in the picker, and fire short sounds into voice from the soundboard, with your own volume and a mute for everyone else's.

New

Bots & Webhooks

Turn on Dice, Polls and Reminders with one click: they run inside your server, nothing to install. Write your own bots with a small Node SDK: /commands that suggest themselves as you type, answers only the asker sees, buttons, menus and forms. A bot reads only what's meant for it unless an admin says otherwise. Paste an Iskra webhook URL into anything that posts to Discord, or send your server's events to your own scripts with signed outgoing webhooks.

Create a server in one click

Hit "Create a server" in the desktop app, pick a starting point (gaming group, friends hangout, board games or empty) and it runs right on your computer, voice included. Tick "Keep my server running" and it starts with your computer. Flip "Let friends anywhere join" and Iskra opens the ports on your router, gets a free address and HTTPS, and friends anywhere can join.

Your server is a folder

Settings, messages, members and uploads all live in one folder, with daily backups. Download an encrypted backup, restore it on another computer and everyone follows your server to its new address. Bringing a community over? Import your Discord channels' history.

Roles & Moderation

Custom roles with colours, icons and permissions, per-channel overrides, private and read-only channels. Block people, time out, kick and ban, slowmode, server mute, AutoMod, reports to a moderators' queue, and an audit log. Every check happens on the server, so nothing slips through a side door.

PC first, phone too

Desktop apps for Windows, macOS and Linux that update themselves, an Android app, and a web app at app.iskra.foo for PC and phone browsers. Add the web app to your home screen and it opens like an app, with push notifications for mentions, DMs and calls. The phone apps keep voice going with the screen locked and notify you while they're open.

Free. Actually free.

Nothing we know how to build sits behind a paywall: not screen share quality, not upload size, not the soundboard, not your profile. No ads. No tracking. Your server and your data stay yours. Iskra runs on donations, and only if you want to.

Next up: an iPhone app in the App Store, DMs with people you share no server with, and more.

No account. No email. No password. Your identity is a key that lives on your own devices. There's no central account service to sign up for, and nothing to go offline.
Identity & privacy

One identity.
Honest encryption.

We only promise what we can actually guarantee. Here's who can read what in Iskra:

Server channels
  • Encrypted on the way to the server
  • Stored on the server you or your friend runs
  • Readable by members and by whoever runs it
  • You know who that is
  • Same for voice and shared files
Direct messages
  • End-to-end encrypted, always, with forward secrecy
  • Only you and your friend can read them
  • Calls too: every frame encrypted on your device
  • libsodium, no home-made crypto
  • No key, no send. Never plain text
  • Safety numbers to check who you're talking to

There's no central relay. DMs, calls and encrypted private channels go through servers you and your friends already use, and those servers only ever handle encrypted messages and media. They can still see who talks to whom and when, and we'd rather tell you than pretend otherwise.

Made on your device

First launch: pick a name. Your key is created quietly in the background. No forms, no verification emails.

Take it to another device

Got a second PC, or want Iskra on your phone? Scan a QR code from a device you're signed in on, check that both show the same number, and you're in. Lost a device? Remove it from your devices list and every server shuts it out.

Your recovery key

Save your recovery key somewhere safe. It's your way back in if a device is lost or wiped. No support ticket, no password reset email.

Friends & safety numbers

Add friends from the servers you share and DM them end-to-end encrypted. Compare safety numbers to check who you're talking to.

Passkeys & key transparency

Lock Iskra and open it with your fingerprint, face or a security key. Each server keeps a signed log of everyone's keys, and your app checks it, so a server that shows different people different keys gets caught.

Profiles & Profile Pages

Your profile.
No paywall.
No ceiling.

Discord charges $10/month for an animated avatar and a banner color. That's the product. That's Nitro.

In Iskra everyone gets an avatar, a bio, pronouns and an accent colour, and on top of that a profile page you write yourself. A custom layout, animations, a pixel-art gallery, your rank in every game, a manifesto. Whatever you actually want to be known for. No feature tiers, no "profile effects" to unlock, no badge shop, no currency.

Write it in the editor built into the app, with a live preview: one HTML file with your own CSS and JavaScript, up to 512 KB. Publish once and it's on every server you're on. Anyone who clicks your name can open it, shown in a strict sandbox.

Discord is a VC-funded product optimizing for engagement and monetization. Iskra is a tool you run yourself. The difference is who's in charge. Here's what you get with Iskra.

  • Your data lives on their servers
  • Screen share quality and big uploads locked behind $10/month Nitro

  • Your server, your machine, your folder

  • DMs and calls end-to-end encrypted, always
  • Profiles, your own profile page and every theme, for everyone

How it works

Up in three steps.

No config files, no signup, no fiddling.

1

Download & create a server

Install the desktop app, click "Create a server" and pick a starting point. It runs on your computer, voice included.

2

Send an invite link

Switch on "Let friends anywhere join" (Server settings → Internet access), then drop the invite link in your group chat.

3

Click & talk

Friends open the link, pick a name, and they're in. Voice, text, screen share. No email, no password.

Hosting

Pick your setup.

Run a server from the desktop app, run the standalone server on Windows, Linux or macOS, or rent a small one. Voice runs alongside your server automatically, so there's no separate TURN server to set up and no shared public one to depend on. Step by step: the hosting guide.

Your server, your choice. Iskra gets nothing from any of this: no hosting fees, no deals with providers. It's just a guide.

A

Desktop app, zero setup

Click "Create a server" in the desktop app, give it a name and pick a starter template: channels, voice channels, roles and rules are ready. It runs on your computer, and friends on your network join with your invite link right away. Tick "Keep my server running" and it starts with your computer, quietly in the tray.

zero setup starter templates starts with your PC
B

One-click internet hosting

Flip "Let friends anywhere join" in Server settings → Internet access, in the desktop app or on the standalone server. Iskra asks your router to open the ports (UPnP or NAT-PMP), gives your server a free address that follows your IP (like 203-0-113-7.sslip.io) and gets a Let's Encrypt certificate for it. A live checklist shows each step. Iskra also tells you what it found out about your connection, from your own router, with no outside service asked. Router won't play along? You get step-by-step port forwarding with your exact addresses and ports, plus a prompt to paste into an AI assistant if you'd like help.

automatic port opening free address automatic HTTPS port forwarding help
C

Your own domain, automatic HTTPS

Run the standalone server (Windows, Linux or macOS), point a domain at it (a free dynamic DNS name works too) and set that domain in server.json. Iskra gets a free Let's Encrypt certificate and renews it on its own. Works for everyone, including the web app on phones. One command sets it to start with the computer, and on Linux it can keep itself up to date.

automatic HTTPS works for everyone your domain
# ports to open 80, 443 # Iskra server, HTTPS 7881/tcp # voice 7882/udp # voice
D

Rent a small server, ready in about 15 minutes

A few euros a month at a VPS provider your group picks, and your server stays up even when your PC is off. The app gives you a setup text to paste when you create the server: it installs Iskra (checked against its published checksum), keeps it running and updated, and sets up HTTPS. Paste the server's address into the app and you're the owner. No terminal needed.

always on one paste no terminal
E

Behind carrier-grade NAT?

Many providers share one internet address between customers, and then nobody outside can reach a server in your home, port forwarding or not. Iskra spots it and lays out your options, quickest first: rent a small server, check whether you already have a public IPv4 address, ask your provider for one, use Tailscale (a free app that links your group's devices), or let a friend with an open connection host. Friends behind CGNAT can always join; only the host needs an open connection.
