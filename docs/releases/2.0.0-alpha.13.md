**Iskra: alpha 13.**

### What's new
- **Updates you can see.** "Restart to update" shows what's happening, the Windows installer shows its progress, and Iskra opens with a small start screen and a progress bar ("Updated to …", "Starting your servers…") that fades into the app.
- **Friends opens on All,** with people who are online first, then busy, away and offline. Online and Pending (requests you sent and ones waiting for you) are one click away.
- **Nothing talks to anyone behind your back.** Videos and posts from YouTube, Twitch, X and Reddit load only when you click (or for sites you choose to always allow), watch parties say where a video plays from first, GIFs come through your own server, and voice no longer asks Google's STUN servers for your address. My settings → Privacy & data lists every outside connection, with a way to turn it off.
- **Strong (RNNoise) is the default noise removal again.** It sounds the most natural today. AI noise removal is one click away under Settings → Voice & audio, and it's much better than before:
- **Your voice sounds like you.** AI noise removal no longer chops or squashes your voice: it still takes out keyboards, fans and clicks, but now leaves speech alone. Choose how hard it works under Settings → Voice & audio → AI noise removal strength (Gentle, Normal or Max). With automatic voice activity your voice is about 100 ms behind while you talk, steady, instead of rushing to catch up. "Even out voices" is gentler and smoother, and the browser's automatic gain no longer fights the noise removal.
- **Restarts you barely notice.** When a server restarts or updates, the app says "<server> is restarting, hold on…" and is back within a second of the server: you're rejoined to voice with the same mute and deafen, and messages you wrote meanwhile are sent. Screen share and camera wait for you to turn them on again. If a server is shut down instead, the app says it went offline.
- **Server settings get their own room.** Right-click a server (or press and hold on a phone) for everything you can do there: invite, create channels, roles, members, reports and more. Server settings open full-window with the server's name, picture and colour on top, so you always know which server you're changing. Your own settings are a separate place.
- **Your profile follows you.** Change your name, picture or page on one device and your other devices pick it up right away. A phone or laptop that was offline can't bring an old name back.
- **Big files in DMs, still end-to-end encrypted.** Send a friend a file as big as your server allows (512 MB by default; the owner can raise it), not just 50 MB. It's encrypted and uploaded a piece at a time, with progress and cancel, and the desktop app, Chrome and Edge save it straight to disk.
- **One name, everywhere.** Change your **name** once and it updates on every server, in DMs and on your profile card. A **server nickname** is optional and only for that server; "Use my name" clears it. A rename now also reaches everyone after a reconnect.
- **Profile pages run their scripts in the desktop app, safely, for everyone.** No more "Run scripts?" prompt: pages run behind a wall where they can't reach the network at all, so a page can never learn your IP address. In browsers and on phones, other people's pages show without scripts (with a note that interactive pages run in the desktop app). Pages can now play sound and video from `data:` URLs.
- **Built-in bots, one click.** Turn on **Dice** (`/roll 2d6+3`), **Polls** (`/poll Pizza or tacos? | Pizza | Tacos`) and **Reminders** (`/remind me in 10m take the pizza out`) in Server settings → Bots & webhooks → Bots. They run inside your server: nothing to install or keep running.
- **Bots that feel at home.** Type `/` and the app suggests every command you can use, with its options. Bots can answer so only you see it, show "thinking…" while they work, and put buttons, menus and forms on their messages. A bot now reads only its commands and the messages meant for it (mentions and replies), unless an admin lets it read everything, when inviting it or later.
- **Outgoing webhooks.** Your server can send chosen events (new messages or reactions in the channels you pick, people joining or leaving, events starting) as signed requests to an address an admin picks: a Minecraft chat bridge, Home Assistant or your own script. Each has a signing secret, a test button and a delivery log, and turns itself off if it keeps failing. Every member sees where their server sends things under Who Iskra talks to.
- **Encrypted channels, tighter.** Every message is now tied to its channel and thread, and every edit to its message, so a server can't move one somewhere else or swap in an older version on a device that saw the newer one. A message that claims to come from someone after they lost access gets a warning and never notifies. Bot commands work there too: the app says first that the command goes to the bot unencrypted, and only you see the answer. If everyone loses access and a channel's encryption starts over, you get a safe, explicit way back in, with a safety-number check.
- **Safer server updates.** A standalone server now checks each release's signed build attestation before installing it, by default, on the server itself. The captions model is given only to members, through a short-lived link.
- **The Mac app updates itself,** like on Windows and Linux, once it's in your Applications folder.
- **Notifications that fit the server.** Until you pick a level, a server with fewer than 15 people notifies you about every message, and a bigger one only about @mentions.
- **Hide or leave.** *Hide server* just takes a server off your list (show it again in My settings → Appearance). *Leave server* really leaves: you're taken off its member list, and you need a new invite to come back.
- **Nothing you write gets lost.** Press Enter while a file is uploading and the message goes out when the upload finishes. Write while a server is away and it's sent when the server is back. Sending fast waits its turn instead of failing, and a message that can't be sent says why.
- **For everyone.** Menus, dialogs and the message list work with the keyboard (arrow keys in the server and channel lists too), screen readers get proper names and announcements, every theme has readable contrast (and role colours stay readable), the theme editor warns about hard-to-read colours, and Iskra moves less when your system asks for reduced motion.
- **Faster.** The web app loads less on first start, the desktop app starts quicker, and busy servers send messages and handle many people reconnecting at once much faster. AI noise removal runs on its own thread, away from the audio itself.
- **GIFs in three steps.** Server settings → GIFs walks an admin through getting a free KLIPY key and turning GIFs on.
- **Your language, everywhere.** Error messages from the server and push notifications now come in English or Swedish too, with many Swedish wording fixes.

### Get started
- **Windows:** `Iskra-Setup-2.0.0-alpha.13.exe`. **Mac:** the `.dmg`. **Linux:** AppImage, `.deb` or `.rpm`. **Android:** the `.apk`.
- **In the browser:** [app.iskra.foo](https://app.iskra.foo).
- **Your own server:** create one in the desktop app, rent one from the app, or run the `iskra-server` package: `iskra-server my-server`, then open the owner link it prints. The [hosting guide](https://github.com/quikmn/iskra-voice/blob/main/docs/hosting.md) has every step.

### Good to know
- **Mac:** the Mac app can only update itself from this version on. Download and install this version once by hand (the `.dmg`), and move Iskra to Applications.
- **Update your apps.** Apps from before this version can't load live captions from an updated server, and can't read new messages in encrypted channels.
- **Bots:** bots that joined before this version keep reading every message until an admin changes it (Server settings → Bots & webhooks → Bots). New bots read only their commands and messages meant for them, unless the admin ticks **Can read all messages**.
- **Notifications:** if you never picked a notification level for a small server (fewer than 15 people), it now notifies you about every message. Pick *Only @mentions* in the server's menu → Notifications to go back.
- **Server owners:** automatic updates now check the build attestation by default (the old `RequireAttestation` setting is ignored). To turn it off, use Server settings → Server updates or `"VerifyAttestation": false` in server.json; you'll see a warning. On Linux with systemd, `iskra-server expect-restart <world>` before a planned restart makes members' apps say "restarting, hold on" instead of "went offline".
- **Outgoing webhook secrets** are kept in the server's database, so they're in its backups. Keep backups private.
